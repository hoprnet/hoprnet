How long a relayer stays dead after a probe went out through it, before its verdict lapses into probation again: one PENDING_SURB_TTL.

A probe is a SURB the counterparty holds and spends oldest-first on reply data like any other, about one stock turnover after it was minted, so every probe through a relayer that is still dead costs the Session one reply. Left on probation, such a relayer was drawn for as long as it stayed dead (hoprnet#8750). Measured on a cluster with the link between the replier and one of its three return relayers cut for six minutes, at 200 replies a second: 1 000 to 1 300 replies lost, four a second from the first probation on, a few percent of a VPN’s downstream, which TCP inside the tunnel answers with a throughput collapse.

The price is paid when the relayer comes back. It is found by the next probe the counterparty spends, so up to one wait later, not within seconds, which is why the wait is no longer than the time a verdict takes to lapse in the first place.
