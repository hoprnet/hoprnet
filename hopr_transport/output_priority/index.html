Priority scheduling of the outgoing traffic classes (session data, probes, noise). Priority scheduling of the three outgoing traffic classes.

Every outgoing packet belongs to one of three lanes: session data, probes and noise (cover traffic). The lanes are merged here, so that the class order is session data first, then probes, then noise, and so that a probe burst cannot delay session packets.

The order comes from two merges:

  1. select_with_strategy merges the session and the probe lane. PacedPollState chooses the session lane for at most max_session_run slots in a row, then yields one probe slot. The probe lane therefore keeps the configured share of the slots while session data is queued, and gets every free slot while it is not.
  2. The result is merged with the noise lane behind a constant preference for the left input, so noise takes a slot only when the session and the probe lane are both empty.

The strategy counts choices, not emissions: when the chosen lane has nothing to yield, select_with_strategy falls through to the other lane, unseen by the counter. Both guarantees hold while both lanes are queued, which is what the tests drive. Transient occupancy just favours probes.

Pongs count as session data. They ride the session lane, because a peer waits on a pong and a late pong records a failed probe on the other side.
