Deterministic core of the PIX session supervisor.

Fields§

§cfg: SupervisorConfig§dims: PixParams§pseudonym: HoprPseudonym§closed: bool§draining: bool

The Session is gone and only its funded front cycle is still being worked for.

Set by on_session_closed and never cleared: a drain ends by closing, one way or another. While it is set the supervisor asks for no successor, plans fill at the ceiling, and stops service-gating the recovery idle deadline — see “Draining a closed Session” in this module’s documentation for why each of those follows from the Session having left rather than being separate policy.

§next_ssa_index: u32§share_order_front: Option<SsaIndex>

The cycle the two share-order counters below are measured against — the earliest unrecovered cycle, i.e. the one the Entry should currently be serving. None before the first batch exists.

§front_useful: u64

Useful shares booked on Self::share_order_front since it took the front.

§off_front_useful: u64

Useful shares booked on any other live cycle over that same span.

off_front_useful / (front_useful + off_front_useful) is the share-order ratio — see SupervisorConfig::max_off_front_share_fraction for what it detects and why it is a fraction rather than a count. Both counters reset when the front changes, which is what keeps the window bounded to a single cycle’s service.

§service_open: bool

Whether the service gate currently follows a funded front cycle.

§service_front: Option<SsaIndex>

Front cycle for which the current gate mode was emitted.

Tracking the identity as well as the mode lets a funded-to-funded handoff rebaseline the served-without-progress ceiling for the newly promoted cycle.

§paid_front_handoff: bool

A front cycle became paid and terminal in one event, so an unfunded successor still earns a freshly restored predeposit allowance even though the gate never entered funded mode.

§paid_recovery_tail: Option<PaidRecoveryTail>

The sole recovered predecessor authorized to report buffered liveness.

This never becomes a set: accepting arbitrary recovered tombstones would let the Entry replay old cycle tags to keep service open. Replacement on each in-order recovery is the supervisor’s independent bound behind the reconstructor’s per-polynomial surplus counters.

§successor_request_deferred: bool

A successor batch was earned but could not yet fit the two-generation reservation.

§resend: Option<PendingResend>

The successor batch that has been requested, has not been answered in full, and is being asked for again.

The Entry admits a successor only once its predecessor’s returned packets have earned the request, and drops it otherwise without a word — so a request sent at the Exit’s early signal is routinely refused, as is one lost on the return path. Without this the Exit waited out the batch’s commitment deadline and lost the batch on it, although the very same request would have been admitted a few seconds later. With the predecessor still live that loss is tolerated rather than fatal: the successor was retired and the Session left with no next cycle, to close on CommitmentTimeout once the predecessor was done.

Unanswered is the interval from a batch’s first SsaRequestSent until every member has reported a CommitmentProgress or a CommitmentVerified. The Entry commits a batch all-or-nothing, but it publishes the members one after another, and a publication cut at a member boundary leaves the later members with no part at all while the earlier ones are complete — so one member’s part shows that the request was admitted and says nothing about the rest. A batch of one is answered by its one part, as it always was.

Behind the front means paid_recovery_tail is set, or the earliest live cycle belongs to another batch — an earlier generation is still being served. It is absorbing: the front only ever advances.

While both hold, handle_deadline emits SessionPixAction::ResendSsaRequest every ssa_request_resend_interval, always naming the whole batch, and each emission pushes the commitment_deadline of every member that has reported nothing to at least now plus the batch-scaled delivery budget. Members that have reported a part keep their own clocks — the re-request timer, then the deposit deadline — which measure a commitment that is under way. So a re-send the Entry admits always has a whole window to deliver in, and a batch that is not yet needed cannot time out. Repeating the whole batch for the members that are done is harmless: the Entry’s repeat branch publishes only the members it still owes. The exception is a repeat that reaches the Entry more than a few seconds after the batch went out in full, which says a burst was lost on the way. The Entry then sends every member’s burst again, without a second ReadyToDeposit, and a member that is done absorbs the copy. That is also what recovers a member whose whole burst was lost, since it has no part a re-request could name. At the front, and for the opening batch, nothing is re-sent and the deadline stops moving: the batch is then lost on CommitmentTimeout at most one window after the last re-send. Never asking at the front also means a request admitted late cannot leave the Entry with a ReadyToDeposit for a cycle the Exit has already given up on.

The same reasoning applies to the ends of a whole Session that the Exit can read off its own clocks. A re-send admitted with less than a delivery window left before one would be answered by an Entry that has been told to fund a cycle the Exit will not be there to reconstruct. There are two, and they differ in whether they can be taken back.

  • The paid tail’s hard deadline, at which handle_deadline closes the Session on RecoveryDeadline outright, is immutable. While it falls inside the window the batch is treated as being at the front, for good: nothing is re-sent, the deadline is not pushed and this state is cleared. It does not close the Session itself — the batch is left to its own deadline, and the Session ends when it was going to anyway. See paid_tail_ends_within.
  • Once the failure allowance is spent, so that the next lost cycle ends the Session, the expiry of any live cycle’s non-service-gated deadline — its commitment, deposit or recovery hard deadline — does, read from every live cycle except the members of this batch that have reported nothing, which the re-send itself pushes. Before the allowance is spent a lost cycle is merely retired, so those deadlines foresee nothing. This one is only a pause: the deadlines are not final, since a reported member’s commitment deadline clears when its commitment verifies and its deposit deadline when it is funded, neither of which ends the Session. While paused nothing is re-sent and nothing is pushed, the timer is set for the next interval and the state is kept, so a later tick looks again and re-sending resumes once the deadline is gone. Ending the re-sends for good would leave a member the Entry still owes unasked, to time out in a Session the re-sends could have saved. If the pause outlasts the deadlines of the members that have reported nothing, they expire, and with the allowance spent that ends the Session as it would have without any re-send. See lost_cycle_ends_session_within.

What cannot be foreseen remains. A re-send already in flight when the Exit gives up on the batch for a reason it cannot schedule — an operator close or drain, a protocol fault, a stall of a service-gated idle clock (the paid tail’s, or a live cycle’s RecoveryIdle, which re-arms instead of closing while no service is consumed and so is no certain end), or a cycle lost for a reason that is not a deadline (its deposit observer closing) — can still be admitted by the Entry after the Exit has torn down, and the Entry then announces a deposit for a cycle that can no longer be reconstructed. The window is one re-send interval plus the transit time of the last re-send; the bounded waste is the price of not letting a refused request time out.

The Entry’s finishing of a publication it was cut short of has the same exposure and a longer window. A cycle whose burst was interrupted is answered, on its next repeat or its next scoped re-request, with a whole burst and the ReadyToDeposit it never emitted. The Exit’s re-request timer asks until just before the cycle’s commitment deadline and does not move it, so a resumed burst — or a repeat that was delayed on the way — can arrive after the Exit has retired the cycle, and the Entry then announces a deposit for an address nobody can reconstruct. That timing is the re-request’s and is unchanged here. A re-send is a second source of the same thing: re-sends go on up to the deadline of a member that has reported a part, which the tick does not push, so the last one — emitted up to one re-send interval before the Exit retires that member and ends the re-sends — can resume and announce it after the fact. Once the failure allowance is spent that member’s deadline is a foreseeable end of the Session and the re-sends pause a window before it; until then losing it only retires it, and the exposure stands.

Cleared when every member has reported a commitment part, when any member is retired, when the carrier reports that it can no longer send this batch, when the batch is found at the front or within a delivery window of the paid tail’s hard deadline, or when a drain begins. A lost cycle that would end the Session pauses it instead and clears nothing. Decided once per batch, on the first SsaRequestSent of whichever member reaches the supervisor first (see resend_decided_batch), so a late or repeated confirmation cannot arm it again once it has been cleared, and a first member that had already reported before its own confirmation does not leave the rest of the batch unarmed.

Unarmed when the interval is zero, which a config that skipped validation can carry; that reads as “off” rather than as a timer that spins.

§resend_decided_batch: Option<u32>

The newest batch whose re-sending has been decided, whether or not it was armed.

Batch ids are monotonic, so this makes the decision once per batch: the first SsaRequestSent of any member settles it, and nothing after that can reopen it — not a late or repeated confirmation, not one that arrives after the state was cleared by a failed result, a retirement, the batch reaching the front or the cut-off. It is set on that first confirmation whether or not the batch armed, which is what keeps a batch the supervisor declined to re-send from being picked up by a later member’s confirmation.

Not tied to the first index, nor to a member’s phase, because neither can be relied on to be the one that reaches the supervisor first: observations can overtake their own confirmation, so the first member may already have left AwaitingCommitment when its confirmation arrives, and a later member’s confirmation can come before it.

§ssas: Vec<PerSsaState>

Ordered SSAs (oldest first, newest last), including short-lived recovered tombstones.

§first_failure_reason: Option<SessionPixCloseReason>

Tracks the first failure reason when multiple SSAs fail, so the earliest cause is used for the final Close action rather than the last.

§failed_cycles: usize

Cycles this Session has lost without recovering them, over its whole life.

Cumulative, and that is the whole point: retiring a failed member leaves no trace on its siblings, so without a counter that survives the retirement an Entry can lose one cycle per batch indefinitely while a single funded sibling holds the Session open. Bounded by SupervisorConfig::max_failed_cycles.

§fill: FillPlanner

Plans the rate at which this Exit originates its own fill keep-alives.

Kept here rather than in the worker because the rate is a function of the lifecycle state this struct owns — which cycle is at the paid front and how far it has got — and because every close path has to be able to stop it in the same action vector that carries the close.

§highest_retired_ssa_index: Option<SsaIndex>

Greatest SSA index that has been retired (closed and removed).

Prevents a stale SsaRequestSent from resurrecting a closed SSA. A high-watermark rather than the set of every retired index: the set grew for the whole life of the Session and was scanned linearly on every request, one entry per cycle, without bound.

A watermark alone would be wrong if it were consulted first — retirement is not monotone, since a later batch member can lose its deposit while an earlier one is still recovering, and “at or below the greatest retired index” would then condemn that live earlier cycle. The guard is therefore placed after the live-record lookup, so a cycle that still exists always wins and the watermark only ever answers for indices that have none.

§telemetry: PixTurnEvents

Cycle lifecycle transitions since the worker last drained this.

Latched rather than emitted because this type is a pure function of its inputs and a metric is not — the same arrangement as FillPlanner’s stall, which is latched here and drained in the worker. Always compiled, including without the telemetry feature, so the default test configuration exercises the counting rather than only the feature-enabled one.

fn refresh_share_order_front(&mut self)

Re-points the share-order accounting at the current front of the batch, clearing it if the front moved.

Clearing on a change of front is what bounds the measurement window to one cycle’s service, and it is why an Entry cannot launder a spree by eventually finishing a cycle: the reset is only reached by the front cycle completing, which is the thing the Exit wanted all along.

Called after every event and every deadline sweep, so it covers both ways the front can move — the front cycle recovering, or being retired.

fn arm_recovery_clocks_for_earliest(&mut self, now: Instant, served_total: u64)

Starts the recovery clocks of the earliest unrecovered cycle, if they are not running yet.

A batch’s cycles are served strictly in index order — the Entry’s emission window is clamped to one cycle (see hopr_protocol_pix::SHARE_EMISSION_WINDOW) — so a cycle behind the front of the batch is queued, not stalled. Starting its clocks when its deposit confirmed, as this used to, measured the queue wait rather than the recovery: at deployed dimensions one cycle takes ~42 min of emission to exhaust, so every cycle after the first was retired by max_recovery_time — or, worse, by max_recovery_idle inside a minute, since the idle gate compares against session-wide service and a queued cycle sees plenty of that without any of it being its own.

So the clock starts when the cycle reaches the front, which is when it can actually make progress, and SupervisorConfig::max_recovery_time then means what its name says: the ceiling on recovering a single cycle. served_total_at_last_progress is re-baselined at the same moment for the same reason — otherwise the idle gate would charge the queue wait against it.

Called after every event and every deadline sweep, so it covers each way a cycle can reach the front: its predecessor recovering, or its predecessor being retired.

pub fn handle_timers( &mut self, now: Instant, served_total: u64, ) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Runs the deadline sweep and, if one is due, the fill tick — in that order.

The order is the point. The sweep is what arms a promoted cycle’s recovery clocks, consumes a spent FIFO tail and re-aligns the service gate, so running it first means the tick plans against the lifecycle as it is now rather than as it was a second ago. A tick that ran first would spend one whole sampling interval filling for a cycle that had already recovered.

This is the entry point the worker uses; handle_deadline remains separate because the deadline logic is meaningful — and tested — on its own.

fn fill_target(&self) -> Option<FillTarget>

The cycle the Exit should currently be filling for, if any.

The recovered predecessor’s FIFO tail wins whenever it exists, for two reasons that point the same way: its SURBs sit ahead of the successor’s in the Exit’s own buffer, so packets sent now carry its shares; and it holds the binding hard deadline, since the successor’s clocks are deliberately not armed until the tail is exhausted (see arm_recovery_clocks_for_earliest).

Otherwise it is the earliest live cycle, and only once it is both funded and clocked. An unfunded cycle has nothing at stake to be filled for, and a funded one whose clocks have not armed is queued behind a predecessor rather than being served — filling for it would spend SURBs on shares the Entry cannot yet be emitting.

fn batch_is_behind_front(&self, batch_id: u32) -> bool

fn next_lost_cycle_ends_session(&self) -> bool

fn paid_tail_ends_within(&self, now: Instant, window: Duration) -> bool

Whether the paid tail’s hard deadline falls within window of now.

handle_deadline closes the Session on RecoveryDeadline the moment the tail’s hard deadline passes, whatever the successor is doing, and nothing that happens meanwhile can take that deadline back: it is immutable. A request admitted with less than a delivery window left before it would be answered by an Entry that is then told to fund a cycle this Exit is no longer there to reconstruct, so the batch’s re-sends stop for good.

The comparison against the batch-scaled window that a re-send promises is inclusive: handle_deadline closes on now >= hard_deadline, so a deadline exactly one window away falls on the very instant the promised window ends, and a re-send admitted at its last moment would race the close. No tail, or a tail without a hard deadline, foresees nothing.

Source

fn lost_cycle_ends_session_within( &self, batch_id: u32, now: Instant, window: Duration, ) -> bool

Whether a lost cycle that would end the Session is due within window of now, so that a request sent now could be admitted only after the Exit has torn down.

True when the failure allowance is spent (next_lost_cycle_ends_session) and some live cycle has a deadline within the window that would cost it. The deadlines are PerSsaState::certain_expiry’s, read from every live cycle except the members of batch_id that have reported nothing — those are the ones the re-send itself pushes, so their deadline is never the end of anything. A member of the batch that has reported a part is not pushed and is counted, as is every cycle of any other batch. Before the allowance is spent a lost cycle is retired and the Session goes on, so the same deadlines foresee nothing.

Unlike paid_tail_ends_within this is only ever a pause. The deadlines it reads are not final: a reported member’s commitment deadline is cleared the moment its commitment verifies, and its deposit deadline when it is funded, and neither ends the Session when that happens. Stopping for good on one would leave the rest of the batch — a member the Entry may still owe — unasked, and let it time out in a Session the re-sends could have saved. The inclusive comparison is the same, for the same reason.

Source

fn resend_can_continue(&self, batch_id: u32, now: Instant) -> bool

Whether the unanswered batch batch_id can still be re-sent at all.

Behind the front — an earlier generation is still being served, which is what the Entry’s gate is waiting on — and the paid tail’s hard deadline not within a delivery window. Both are permanent: the front only advances and the tail’s deadline never moves, so where either fails the batch is treated exactly as one that has reached the front. Nothing is re-sent, its deadline stops moving, and it is left to that deadline. This is deliberately not lost_cycle_ends_session_within, which can pass.

Source

fn resend_is_paused(&self, batch_id: u32, now: Instant) -> bool

Whether the unanswered batch batch_id’s re-sends are paused at now: a lost cycle that would end the Session is due within a delivery window.

While paused nothing is sent and no deadline is pushed, and the state is kept so that a later tick looks again. If the pause outlasts the deadlines of the members that have reported nothing, they expire — and, the allowance being spent, that ends the Session, as it would have without re-sends. The pause costs nothing that was not already lost; ending the re-sends would.

Source

fn delivery_window(&self) -> Duration

The time a batch is given to deliver its commitment: max_ssa_delivery_time, batch-scaled.

Source

fn batch_is_answered(&self, batch_id: u32) -> bool

Whether every member of batch batch_id has reported a part of its commitment.

The batch is answered only then. The Entry commits a batch all-or-nothing, but it publishes it one member after another, and a publication cut short at a member boundary leaves the later members with nothing at all while the earlier ones are complete — so one member’s part proves that the request was admitted and nothing about the members after it.

Source

fn arm_ssa_request_resend(&mut self, batch_id: u32, now: Instant)

Starts re-sending the batch batch_id, whose first confirmation has just arrived, if it should be.

Called once per batch, on the first confirmation of whichever member reaches the supervisor first (see resend_decided_batch): a batch is confirmed one index at a time, and one timer per batch is the point. The conditions are the whole of the policy. A zero interval is “off”; a drain has no Session left to serve a successor; a batch whose every member has already reported a commitment part was answered before its own confirmation was processed, which the ordering of the two does not rule out; and a batch at the front, or one the paid tail’s hard deadline would outrun (see resend_can_continue), is not asked for again, so the opening batch never is.

A lost cycle that would end the Session is deliberately not consulted here (see resend_is_paused). It only pauses the re-sends, so the tick is the one to decide it — and the decision here is final, which a pause is not. A member that reported before its own confirmation has just been given a deadline exactly one window out, and consulting it would leave a batch unarmed for good on a deadline that is about to clear.

fn clear_resend_for(&mut self, batch_id: u32)

Stops re-sending batch_id, if that is the batch being re-sent.

fn note_commitment_seen(&mut self, idx: usize)

Records that part of the cycle at idx’s commitment arrived, and ends the re-sends of its batch once every member has said as much.

One member’s part does not end them: it shows the request was admitted, not that the Entry got as far as the members after it (see batch_is_answered). Until the last member reports, the whole batch keeps being re-sent. The Entry’s repeat branch publishes only the members it still owes, so repeating for the ones that are done costs nothing until the repeats outlast the Entry’s grace. From then on the Entry takes the bursts to have been lost and sends every member’s again, at the cost of one commitment retransmission each.

Source

fn tick_ssa_request_resend(&mut self, now: Instant) -> Option<SessionPixAction>

Re-sends the unanswered successor batch if its turn has come, pushing its deadlines with it.

Stops instead — clearing the state, so nothing re-arms it — when the batch has reached the front, when the paid tail’s hard deadline is within a delivery window (see paid_tail_ends_within), when a drain has begun, when every member has reported a part, or when a member is missing or — having reported nothing — has left AwaitingCommitment. Those are the same facts the clears at the event handlers act on, checked again here because this is the only place that acts on the absence of an event: the front moving, or a deadline drawing near, is not one.

Pauses instead — sending and pushing nothing, setting the timer for the next interval and keeping the state — while a lost cycle that would end the Session is due within a delivery window (see lost_cycle_ends_session_within). That deadline can clear without the Session ending, and the decision to re-send a batch is made only once, so stopping for good on it would be final on the strength of something that may not happen.

A member that has reported a part is no longer waiting for the request to be admitted, and may have moved on to its deposit or beyond, so it is not a reason to stop — the batch is re-sent whole for as long as some other member has heard nothing.

The commitment_deadline of every member that has heard nothing becomes the later of what it was and now plus the batch-scaled delivery budget, so the budget is never shortened and a re-send the Entry admits always finds a whole window ahead of it. Members that have reported a part are left on their own clocks — the re-request timer and then the deposit deadline — which are measuring a commitment that is under way and are not this timer’s to extend. The deadline is moved here, when the action is emitted, rather than when it is carried out: the carrier may drop the action or fail to send it, and the batch is then no worse off than if the Entry had refused it.

Source

fn on_commitment_progress( &mut self, ssa_id: &SsaId<HoprPseudonym>, now: Instant, ) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Arms — or re-arms — the re-request timer on a commitment that is under way but not complete.

This is where the issue’s rule lives: a cycle that received nothing never reaches here, so it never asks, which is correct because there is no scope for it to name. And because every arriving part pushes the timer out, the first ask cannot land in the middle of the burst — what the timer measures is a stall in delivery.

A cycle that received nothing is not necessarily left to its deadline, though: a successor batch that is still behind the front is asking the Entry for the whole commitment again, see resend. The first part reported here ends that for this cycle — the request was admitted, and the missing parts are this timer’s to chase from then on — and the last member’s first part ends it for the batch.

Unscaled by the batch size, unlike the two absolute deadlines: this is a per-cycle stall detector, and a batch’s commitment sets do not queue behind one another in a way that would make one cycle’s silence expected.

fn on_deposit_confirmed( &mut self, ssa_id: &SsaId<HoprPseudonym>, amount: HoprBalance, now: Instant, served_total: u64, ) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Funds the cycle on the deposit pool’s verdict. The supervisor does not second-guess the amount.

Sufficiency is priced and judged outside this workspace. The Exit’s pool is handed the DepositUpdated sender together with the byte quota when the deposit address arrives, and it sends on that channel once the deposit clears the price it charges for that quota — so by the time this runs, the question has been answered by the only component that can answer it. Nothing in the Session layer prices a byte.

This used to compare the running total against max(expected_deposit, min_deposit). Both are gone, and not because they were merely unused: a configured floor here is not a backstop behind the pool but a second authority alongside it, and one set above the pool’s price fails a cycle that has already been paid for — silently, on max_deposit_wait, with neither side able to break the tie. expected_deposit was the same idea arriving over the wire, and the commitment never carried an amount to fill it.

What remains is the one check that needs no price: a zero balance asserts that nothing was deposited, so it is not a verdict and cannot release service. The deadline keeps running and a later confirmation can still fund the cycle.

fn on_unverifiable_shares( &mut self, ssa_id: &SsaId<HoprPseudonym>, observed_total: u64, peer: &OffchainPublicKey, _now: Instant, ) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Closes the Session on the first unverifiable-share report. There is no tolerance to configure.

Shares are not checked on arrival, so a report is not “a bad share” — it means a whole polynomial’s share set failed to open its commitment, which surfaces only once threshold of them have been interpolated. SsaPart::add_share then marks that part failed and releases its shares, and nothing ever clears the flag; since the SSA is the sum of every polynomial’s constant term, the cycle can no longer be reconstructed by any means and will never pay.

So serving on is not tolerance, it is donation: every packet past this point is unpaid with no recovery to preserve at the end of it. That holds however the failure arose, which is why this is not a knob — a false positive from a verification bug leaves the part just as permanently failed, so a tolerance would buy unpaid service rather than the cycle it was raised to save. Closing here caps the exposure at the threshold packets already served rather than a multiple of it.

observed_total is the reconstructor’s cross-peer aggregate, and with no limit to compare it against it is logged rather than accumulated: it says how many polynomials the batch that triggered this took down, which is the difference between one bad relayer and a peer sending garbage wholesale.

Note this closes the Session directly rather than through close_ssa_and_collect, so a batch’s surviving siblings do not keep it alive: unlike a lost deposit, this fault is evidence about the peer rather than about one cycle.

Source

fn on_session_closed( &mut self, available: u64, _now: Instant, ) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Decides whether a Session closed at the Exit is worth draining its buffered SURBs into.

available is the manager’s estimate of the SURBs still spendable, already net of the fill reserve. The cycle it is measured against is the earliest live one, and only when it is both funded and clocked — i.e. exactly the cycle fill would have been working for. Two shapes have nothing a drain could buy and close at once: an unfunded front, where no deposit is at stake; and a recovered predecessor’s paid FIFO tail, whose key is already recovered and behind which the successor is queued without clocks of its own.

The comparison is against the whole remaining emission, with no loss margin. That is conservative in both directions on purpose: the estimate is an upper bound (see SessionPixEvent::SessionClosed), while in-flight packets are already subtracted from it and full recovery in practice needs fewer than E shares. A drain that runs out partway has spent the Session’s SURBs down to the reserve and recovered nothing, which is strictly worse than never starting.

Siblings are retired directly rather than through close_ssa_and_collect, which would be wrong twice over: it charges failed_cycles, and these cycles were retired by an operator closing a Session rather than lost by an Entry; and its ssas.len() == 1 branch would close the Session outright. Recovered tombstones are left alone — they hold no reconstructor state and expire on their own timer with an idempotent RetireSsa.

_now is taken for symmetry with every other handler; nothing here is timed. A drain inherits the target’s existing clocks rather than starting any of its own, which is the point — the resource bound on the cycle must not be extended by the Session ending.

fn emit_request_next_ssa(&mut self, now: Instant) -> Vec<SessionPixAction>

Allocates the next batch of SSA indices and asks for all of them in one action.

The batch size is SupervisorConfig::ssas_per_request, clamped rather than trusted since a supervisor can be built from a config that never went through validate_pix_supervision.

Exactly one cycle of the batch — the last one allocated — carries PerSsaState::is_batch_last, and only that cycle may ask for the successor batch. Without it every member would ask for one of its own, since the request flags are per-cycle; see that field for why the last rather than the first.

Before allocating, this enforces both halves of the admission reservation: no more than crate::MAX_OVERLAPPING_BATCHES live generations and no more than that many full batches’ live cycles. A request earned while the reservation is full is retried after a generation releases its reconstructor state.

What batching does change is the exposure within a batch: every cycle in it is unfunded at once, so the ceiling is ssas_per_request SSA quotas rather than one. That is the trade the knob exists to make, and it is why both deadlines are scaled by the same factor.
