acquire_egress_permit for a packet of the slot instance, which is refused once the instance is superseded.

Checked twice by the callers’ design: before the packet is counted (the caller’s own instance.is_superseded() test, ahead of any accounting), and here once the gate has admitted a parked packet. A writer parked on the gate while its slot is torn down is woken by the gate’s poisoning with an error, but a permit granted in the same breath must not slip through either. The synchronous admission has no suspension between the caller’s check and the permit, so it needs no second look. A cut packet gives back the SURB its sender counted.

One cost is left, and accepted as bounded: a writer parked on the gate that the gate then admits has been counted as served by the gate (its served counter, and its predeposit allowance while unfunded), though its packet is cut and no progress follows. Across a takeover the gate is kept, so the old plane’s parked writer costs the new one a single unit of its served-without-progress ceiling. A sink drives one send at a time, so a plane has at most one such writer, once per takeover, against a ceiling of thousands of packets (max_served_without_progress). A gate operation that undid the count would have to do so across a mode change and a progress snapshot without ever being able to over-credit, which is a worse trade than one packet.
