pub fn tolerated_share_loss(polys: u16, threshold: u8, surplus: u8) -> f64
Expand description

The largest per-share loss rate at which a cycle of polys polynomials, each emitting threshold + surplus shares, still recovers at 99 % — the target default_surplus_for sizes the surplus to, so the deployed split tolerates just over the 50 % it was derived for.

Shares are taken to be lost independently. A polynomial survives with probability P(Binom(threshold + surplus, 1 − loss) >= threshold), the cycle with that raised to the power polys, and the result falls monotonically as the loss rises; the rate where it crosses the target is found by bisection, to within 2⁻⁶⁰.

Not surplus/(threshold + surplus). That is the break-even ratio: the loss at which a polynomial receives exactly threshold shares on average. Around half of all polynomials fall short of their average, and a cycle needs every one of them, so a cycle at break-even fails. At the deployed 4096 × (32 + 79) the ratio is 71.2 % and this is 50.3 %; a cycle losing 71.2 % of its shares recovers with probability indistinguishable from zero.

Evaluated in log space, which is what lets one implementation serve the whole accepted range. The recurrence default_surplus_for uses starts from the all-lost term loss^(threshold + surplus) and multiplies its way up from there; at 510 shares that term underflows to zero for any loss below about a quarter and takes every later term with it, while the tolerances the bisection has to reach in that corner run down to 10⁻⁹. Here each term is a logarithm, the tail is a log-sum-exp, and the cycle probability is exp(polys · ln(1 − tail)) rather than a power of 1 − tail, whose subtraction keeps only ten or so of the tail’s digits at the deployed split.

Not const, because ln and exp are not, and nothing needs it to be: this prices service at runtime rather than defining a constant.

A cycle with no polynomials, or polynomials with a zero threshold, recovers whatever is lost, and tolerates a loss of one.
