pub const fn default_surplus_for(polys: u16, threshold: u8) -> u8
Expand description

Shares to emit per polynomial beyond threshold, to absorb losses: the fewest with which a cycle of polys polynomials still recovers at 99 % when every share is lost independently with probability one half.

A polynomial reconstructs from the first threshold of the threshold + surplus shares emitted for it, so it survives with probability P(Binom(threshold + surplus, 1/2) >= threshold). A cycle recovers only if every one of its polys polynomials does — the SSA is the sum of their constant terms — so that probability is raised to the power polys, and the surplus depends on the polynomial count as well as on the threshold.

This replaced a fixed ratio, threshold / 4, documented as covering 20 % share loss. It covered 20 % per polynomial; with the exponent applied, a cycle of 4096 tolerated about 3 %, and the former 8192 × (64 + 16) about 5 %. Deriving from the target also gives every split the same tolerance, where any fixed ratio gives each a different one.

The required ratio falls as the threshold rises — at 4096 polynomials it is 52 surplus shares at threshold 16 (3.3×), 79 at 32 (2.5×), 126 at 64 (2.0×) and 211 at 128 (1.6×) — because a larger sample concentrates around its mean.

Saturates at 255, the byte the surplus travels in. From a threshold of around 160 the target needs more than that, and the result is then the most the wire can carry rather than a surplus meeting the target.

const so that DEFAULT_SURPLUS_SHARES can be defined by it, and deterministic for the same reason: it is evaluated with IEEE f64 addition, multiplication and division only, whose results are fixed.
